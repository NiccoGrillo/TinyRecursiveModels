# Two viable worlds, frozen conditional execution · 28 November 2026

Question: Can the frozen native checkpoint reconsider a shared belief after receiving either of two viable assumptions, predict the different consequences outside the supplied cell, and preserve the facts common to both? The older unique-board digit edit could assign an impossible antecedent. Here both alternate facts are valid. The two assignments are supplied by the task, not discovered by the model. This tests reasoning under an assumption and amortization when two requested conditionals share a prefix. It does not test cross-branch fusion.

Use the pinned 8,192 original training-source puzzles. Exclude prior matched original/dev/held-out membership and all 256 intervention IDs. Rank eligible parents by SHA256("ph4-master/"+ID); take the last 2,048 in ascending rank order as the source. Generate up to 256 eligible pairs, at most one per parent. In each of four seed-fixed random clue removal orders, remove clues until a first non-unique board is found; accept only exactly two completions disagreeing at the last removed clue. Exhaustively enumerate with limit three and a 300,000-node cap per call; a capped proof cannot qualify. Verify both completed grids and confirm both conditional inputs are uniquely solvable in a second pass. Record exclusions and counterfactual size, do not use the neural model to select. These are derived development cases, not standard Sudoku-Extreme accuracy. Each parent remains one unit; checkpoint training overlap is possible.

Run native TRM with full legal inputs. Warm model first runs eight macros on the ambiguous board then forks the shared state; each fork gets its own assumed digit in the input on EVERY one of the next eight macros. Independent cold solves get their respective assumed digit from initialization for 12 macros each. Both policies spend 24 macros across the two worlds. Also measure cold at 8 and 16, and the 16-macro unchanged-context rival given the answer at the pivot only after its calculation. It cannot solve both different boards by copying. Two mechanistic interventions reset only the answer or only the workspace to the native initializer at the warm fork, then run the same eight informed macros per world. Each is a separate policy charged 24 macros. These are diagnostic activations, not trained architectures.

Primary endpoint: fraction of parent pairs where BOTH warm predictions solve their full world exactly. Main resource comparison: paired warm minus cold-12, with conservative 95% paired interval. Behavioral evidence requires at least 10 complete pairs with both assignments correct and a positive exact lower bound versus blind pivot-only copying. A shared-work accuracy advantage requires the lower bound vs cold-12 above zero. Record all off-pivot differing cells correct in both worlds and accuracy on invariant blanks, separate near/far (sharing a row, column or box with pivot), original and alternate solve counts, and both-world results for pairs with >=3 distinct digits among changes. No selector can see verifier completions or analytic masks at inference; generation alone sees the enumerator. No opportunistic macro-depth selection. A hard-coded exact solver is the domain-specific ceiling; time the independent unique-world solver separately including its search. Positive conditional behavior demonstrates use of input assumptions; it does not establish a human-like proof or efficiency over specialized search.
